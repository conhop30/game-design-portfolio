Homebrew System

Sanctums

When it comes to player-driven systems, the focus has always been baked into the character sheet and domain cards. With Sanctums, we stray a little off of the character sheet and domain cards, while still staying anchored to the player-driven fantasy.

System

Sanctum

A place an adventurer can call home.

The goal for Sanctums is to give a player a mechanical way to commit to a specific domain from their selection. This system could help a player really dive into the fantasy of being a shadow-steeped Rogue or a studied Bard.

Process

Steps to Sanctums

01

Step 1

Establishing a Sanctum

Starting at Tier 2, a player and GM can work together on when a Sanctum is established. This could be a castle, a tavern, or a wandering group of nomads who are a safe rendevous point wherever the adventure is. Upon choosing a Sanctum, the player gains the Sanctum benefits after their next long rest.

02

Step 2

New Downtime Move — Upkeep

After the long rest, the player obtains a new downtime move called "Upkeep". The player instantiates an Upkeep Countdown (6) that starts at 3. On a rest, a player that uses the Upkeep downtime move rolls a 1d4, counting up on the Upkeep Countdown, up to 6. If a player doesn't take the Upkeep downtime move, then it counts down 1. Should it ever reach 0, the Sanctum becomes temporarily Dilapidated, which will be discussed in Step 4.

03

Step 3

Facilities

At Tiers 3 and Tier 4, you can attach a "Facility" to your Sanctum. These act as minor boons to a player's character that are passively powerful. This helps to ensure the power is felt, but the Cognitive Load is not.

04

Step 4

Dilapidation

A Countdown 0 on the Upkeep Countdown means that the player neglected their Sanctum to the point of disaster. Potential consequences could include:

  • Permanently placing a domain card into the vault.
  • Permanently destroying a Facility.
  • Gaining a temporary Scar.
  • Temporarily losing a Loadout Slot until the next level up, or until the GM deems otherwise if at level 10.
  • Temporarily increase Recall Costs by 1.

In order to remove the Dilapidated condition, the player and the GM can work out a cost; A short adventure to find the necessary materials, a total of 12 Hope spent over time, or a permanent Scar for near immediate restoration. Once the Dilapidated condition is removed, the Upkeep Countdown is set to 2.
